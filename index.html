Product design case study Concept · 2025

Bud - Your Finance Buddy

Making budgeting simple, approachable, and sustainable for young adults navigating everyday finances.

Goal-based saving Financial health score Smart spending alerts
01
Overview

For many young adults entering the workforce, managing money can feel overwhelming. Between paying rent, splitting bills, dining out, subscriptions, and saving for future goals, it’s easy to lose track of where money goes each month. While budgeting apps exist, many feel overly complex, requiring constant manual tracking and financial knowledge that can discourage consistent use.

BUD is a conceptual budgeting app designed for young adults aged 24–30 who want to manage their finances with confidence but often feel overwhelmed by traditional budgeting methods. Instead of acting like a financial tracker, BUD acts as a supportive finance buddy helping users understand their spending, save for meaningful purchases, and develop healthier financial habits through simple, proactive guidance.

The goal wasn’t just to help users budget it was to make managing money feel approachable, rewarding, and sustainable.

02
The problem

Many people know they should budget, but few are able to maintain the habit consistently. Through observing common budgeting behaviors and existing financial products, I identified several recurring challenges:

  • Budgeting often feels complicated and time-consuming.
  • Users struggle to understand where their money disappears each month.
  • Saving for larger purchases feels difficult because the entire cost seems intimidating.
  • Unexpected overspending often goes unnoticed until it’s too late.

The result is a cycle of financial stress where users feel guilty about spending but lack the tools to confidently manage their money.

03
The opportunity
The old ask

Become a financial expert

The reframe

Trust a companion that simplifies the decision

Instead of asking users to become financial experts, what if the app could become a trusted companion that simplifies financial decisions? The goal was to create a budgeting experience that feels approachable, encouraging, and easy to maintain, helping users make gradual progress rather than demanding perfection.

05
Design goals

The product was guided by four key objectives.

Reduce the feeling of financial overwhelm

Strip the experience back to what a person actually needs to decide something today.

Make budgeting easy to understand at a glance

Financial position should be readable in seconds, not assembled from several screens.

Encourage consistent saving through achievable goals

Break large purchases into contributions small enough to keep up with.

Help users stay proactive instead of reacting after overspending

Surface a problem while there is still time to act on it.

07
Solution

BUD combines budgeting, expense tracking, savings goals, and timely reminders into a single, easy-to-understand experience.

Rather than overwhelming users with financial data, the app surfaces the information that matters most, allowing users to quickly understand their financial health and take action before small problems become larger ones.

09
Screens 

Onboarding

Most finance apps open with a form. BUD opens with a conversation: a gentle, judgment-free intake that learns how someone actually relates to money before it shows them a single number.

  • A calm welcome that sets expectations, three minutes, private, skippable.
  • Fourteen questions across the basics, habits, outlook and feelings.
  • A short instinct quiz that reads behaviour rather than knowledge.
  • A money personality result that sets the app's tone and first priorities.

Every question carries a "prefer not to answer" escape, so the flow never becomes a wall. By the end BUD knows what to focus on first, and the user has already had one honest moment with their money.

Goal-Based Saving

One of the biggest barriers to saving is the psychological weight of a large purchase. Instead of expecting users to save the entire amount at once, BUD allows them to create a savings goal for something they want to buy, such as a phone, vacation, or laptop.

  • Selects the item they want to purchase.
  • Enters its price.
  • Chooses how many months they would like to save.
  • BUD automatically calculates how much should be set aside each month.

Breaking large purchases into smaller monthly contributions makes saving feel realistic and achievable while reducing impulsive spending.

Financial Health Dashboard

Users shouldn’t need to interpret complicated charts to understand their finances. The home dashboard provides an instant overview of their financial situation.

  • Account balance
  • Total monthly spending
  • Remaining budget
  • Amount saved

This gives users immediate clarity about where they stand financially without requiring multiple screens or calculations.

Spending Breakdown

Understanding spending patterns is the first step toward improving them. BUD categorizes expenses into familiar groups.

  • Food
  • Groceries
  • Utilities
  • Mobile Recharge
  • Transportation
  • Entertainment

Instead of showing raw transaction lists, the app provides an easy-to-read breakdown, helping users identify spending habits and make informed decisions.

11
Design principles

Throughout the project, every design decision was guided by four principles.

Simplicity over complexity

Financial information should be understandable within seconds.

Progress over perfection

Small, consistent improvements are more sustainable than expecting perfect budgeting habits.

Reduce financial anxiety

The experience should feel supportive rather than judgmental.

Actionable insights

Every screen should help users understand what they can do next instead of simply presenting data.

12
Expected experience

By combining visual clarity with proactive financial guidance, BUD aims to help users:

  • Feel more confident managing their money.
  • Build consistent saving habits.
  • Stay aware of spending before problems arise.
  • Reduce financial anxiety through better visibility and planning.
  • Achieve long-term financial goals through small, manageable steps.
13
Reflection

This project challenged me to think beyond creating visually appealing interfaces and instead focus on designing behaviors that encourage healthier financial habits.

Rather than treating budgeting as a tracking exercise, I explored how thoughtful product design could make money management feel approachable, supportive, and motivating. The process reinforced the importance of reducing cognitive load, presenting information progressively, and designing experiences that help users feel empowered rather than overwhelmed.

If I were to continue developing BUD, I would validate these concepts through user testing, explore personalized budgeting recommendations, and investigate how AI-driven insights could make financial guidance even more proactive and tailored to individual spending patterns.

Blossom, Product design case study

A calm companion for the nine longest months

Designing a calm and supportive pregnancy companion for expectant mothers.

Role
Product Designer
Project type
Conceptual Product Design
Duration
4 weeks
Tools
Figma, FigJam
01

Overview

Pregnancy is a transformative experience filled with excitement, anticipation, and uncertainty. While there are numerous pregnancy apps available today, many of them overwhelm users with excessive information, complex navigation, and fragmented experiences.

Blossom was designed as a digital companion that supports women throughout their pregnancy journey by bringing together health tracking, educational content, appointment management, and community support in a single, easy-to-use platform.

02

The Problem

Expectant mothers often rely on multiple sources to manage their pregnancy.

Health and medication tracking apps
Medical appointment reminders
Pregnancy articles and educational resources
Online forums and communities

Switching between different platforms can create unnecessary cognitive load during a physically and emotionally demanding period.

Many existing pregnancy applications also present large amounts of information at once, making it difficult for users to focus on what matters most at their current stage of pregnancy.

How might we create a pregnancy companion that helps expectant mothers stay informed, organized, and emotionally supported without overwhelming them?

03

Understanding Users

Through secondary research and analysis of existing pregnancy products, several recurring needs emerged.

Stay informed

Users want reliable information relevant to their current stage of pregnancy rather than generic content.

Stay organized

Managing medications, supplements, doctor visits, and pregnancy milestones can become difficult without a centralized system.

Feel reassured

Many mothers experience anxiety throughout pregnancy and seek guidance that helps them feel confident and prepared.

Feel connected

Pregnancy can sometimes feel isolating. Users value spaces where they can interact with others going through similar experiences.

04

Product Goals

The solution needed to:

Simplify pregnancy management.
Reduce information overload.
Help users track important health-related activities.
Deliver personalized pregnancy insights.
Foster a sense of emotional support and community.
Create a calm and approachable user experience.
05

Competitive Analysis

While reviewing existing pregnancy applications, I observed that many products focused heavily on information delivery but often lacked simplicity.

Key opportunities
Present only the most relevant information.
Create a cleaner and less cluttered interface.
Centralize essential pregnancy tools.
Make progress and baby development easy to understand at a glance.

These opportunities became the foundation for Blossom.

06

Defining the Experience

Instead of designing another feature-heavy pregnancy app, I focused on creating a supportive companion experience. Three design principles guided the product.

Simplicity First

Users should never feel overwhelmed by information or navigation.

Personalized Guidance

Content and progress tracking should adapt to the user’s pregnancy stage.

Emotional Support

The product should feel reassuring and supportive, not clinical or intimidating.

07

10

Key Learnings

01
Designing for emotional states is as important as designing for tasks

Pregnancy is not only a health journey but also an emotional one. Users need reassurance alongside functionality.

02
Less can be more

Removing unnecessary complexity helped create a more focused and approachable experience.

03
Contextual information improves engagement

Providing information that matches a user’s pregnancy stage makes content feel more useful and relevant.

11

Final Outcome

Blossom is a pregnancy companion designed to help expectant mothers navigate pregnancy with greater confidence, clarity, and support.

By combining health tracking, educational resources, appointment management, baby development insights, and community engagement into a single intuitive experience, Blossom transforms pregnancy management into a calmer and more meaningful journey.

The project demonstrates my approach to solving user problems through research-driven thinking, clear information architecture, and thoughtful user-centered design.

Blossom, because the person carrying a whole life deserves to feel supported too.
